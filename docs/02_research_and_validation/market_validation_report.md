## 1. Executive Summary

BecomingOS should be positioned as an educational, non-clinical personal operating system for self-discovery, values clarification, behaviour alignment, daily action, AI-assisted reflection, and user-confirmed memory.

The market opportunity is plausible but not yet validated. The repository contains planning and readiness artifacts, but no live demand data, pricing evidence, conversion data, willingness-to-pay data, or completed competitor analysis. All market conclusions in this report are assumptions unless explicitly validated later through experiments.

The primary validation question is whether target users want a structured, privacy-conscious reflection and action system enough to join a waitlist, complete a prototype flow, and potentially pay for an MVP without confusing the product with therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis support, coaching replacement, legal advice, financial advice, or medical advice.

Recommended MVP monetisation posture: begin with a low-risk individual subscription or founder-plan test only after positioning and consent language pass legal/privacy review. Do not sell therapeutic outcomes, mental-health treatment outcomes, crisis support, or guaranteed life improvement.

## 7. Therapy/Coaching Confusion Risk

This is a major market and safety risk.

High-risk interpretation patterns:

- Users may think BecomingOS provides therapy, diagnosis, treatment, or mental-health support.
- Users may expect the product to handle crisis disclosures or emotional emergencies.
- Users may treat AI-generated reflections as authoritative advice.
- Users may think the product replaces a therapist, coach, doctor, lawyer, financial advisor, or other qualified professional.
- Users may interpret behaviour alignment as prescription or high-impact decision guidance.

Required mitigations:

- Use non-clinical, educational language throughout acquisition, onboarding, and product surfaces.
- Make AI outputs draft-only and user-confirmed.
- Avoid outcome guarantees and claims about fixing, healing, diagnosing, treating, or replacing professional support.
- Include clear unsupported-use boundaries before validation experiments.
- Route crisis-related language to external resources according to the crisis localisation policy, without storing raw crisis text by default.

- Diagnose your patterns.

- Replace therapy, coaching, medical care, legal advice, or financial advice.
- Crisis support or emergency help.

## 10. MVP Monetisation Recommendation

Recommended test sequence:

1. Start with a no-payment landing page and waitlist test to validate positioning, segment interest, and confusion risk.
2. Add a paid-intent survey or refundable founder-plan expression of interest only after legal/privacy-approved copy exists.
3. Use concierge prototype sessions to understand perceived value before building billing infrastructure.
4. Defer subscription implementation until waitlist, interview, and prototype completion signals justify MVP build.

Recommended initial monetisation hypothesis:

> Individual users may pay for a structured, privacy-conscious reflection and action system if it clearly avoids therapy/coaching replacement claims and demonstrates recurring value through daily action and memory review loops.

This is not validated yet.

## 15. Waitlist Experiment Plan

Objective: determine whether target users will express real interest before MVP build.

Minimum data to collect:

- Email address or contact consent.
- Segment self-identification.
- Primary reason for joining.
- Current alternatives used.
- Interest in research interview or prototype test.
- Optional pricing sensitivity question.

Privacy requirements:

- Provide clear consent language.
- State how waitlist data will be used.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal disclosures unless explicitly approved.
- Allow withdrawal or deletion request handling according to privacy guidance.

Potential success indicators:

- Meaningful signup conversion from targeted traffic.
- Qualitative responses that match intended non-clinical use cases.
- Low rate of therapy/treatment/crisis confusion.
- Enough interview volunteers to run prototype research.

## 16. Concierge Prototype Experiment Plan

Objective: test whether users understand and value the BecomingOS workflow before production implementation.

Prototype flow to test:

1. Consent and boundary confirmation.
2. Guided values or self-discovery module.
3. Reflective question sequence.
4. AI-assisted draft interpretation presented as editable and rejectable.
5. User confirmation before anything is treated as memory.
6. Manual daily action or Today directive planning.
7. Debrief on usefulness, clarity, trust, and willingness to continue.

Key observations:

- Do users complete the flow?
- Do they understand AI outputs are drafts?
- Do they feel in control of memory?
- Do they confuse the experience with therapy, diagnosis, treatment, or coaching replacement?
- Do they want to repeat the experience?
- Would they pay for a productised version?

## 18. Go/No-Go Criteria

Go toward MVP planning only if:

- Target users understand the product as educational, reflective, structured, user-controlled, and privacy-conscious.
- Demand experiments show meaningful interest from intended segments.
- Prototype users complete core flows and report practical usefulness.
- Confusion with therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis support, or professional advice is low and mitigated.
- Pricing research shows at least directional willingness to pay.
- Privacy, legal, safety, and research owners approve the validation approach and findings.
- Integration Agent later confirms all required evidence exists.

No-go or pause if:

- Users primarily interpret the product as therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis support, or replacement for a therapist/coach.
- Interest depends on prohibited claims or high-impact advice.
- Users do not understand or trust user-confirmed memory.
- Demand signals are weak or limited to curiosity without repeat intent.
- Privacy/legal/safety owners identify unresolved P0 risks.
- Evidence is missing, anecdotal, or not tied to documented research.
