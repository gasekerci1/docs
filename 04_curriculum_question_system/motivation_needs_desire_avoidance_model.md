# Motivation, Needs, Desire, Avoidance, And Future-Self Model

Current status: educational and reflective planning model. This document does not approve public MVP build, production readiness, therapy, diagnosis, treatment, guaranteed change, crisis intervention, or professional advice.

Primary research source: `03_research_validation/evidence_based_growth_systems_report.md`.

## Purpose

This model explains how BecomingOS may help users reflect on why an action matters, what supports or blocks it, and how it connects to a future self. It is not a clinical model and must not be used to diagnose users.

## Core Concepts

| Concept | Meaning in BecomingOS | Product boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomous motivation | The user experiences the action as personally endorsed rather than externally imposed. | BecomingOS may invite choice and rationale; it must not pressure, coerce, or shame. |
| Competence | The user feels capable of taking a next step. | BecomingOS may scale actions down and support small wins; it must not promise guaranteed change. |
| Relatedness | The user may feel supported by trusted people. | Support Circle must be optional, permissioned, and user-controlled. |
| Values | User-stated priorities and reasons that make an action meaningful. | Values are reflective context, not a moral score or diagnosis. |
| Desire | What the user wants to move toward. | Desire should be explored as user-authored context, not imposed by AI. |
| Avoidance | What the user may be trying to avoid, protect against, or reduce. | Avoidance can be reflected on non-clinically; do not infer trauma, pathology, or addiction. |
| Future-self alignment | The connection between today's action and the future identity or life direction the user endorses. | Future-self work should end in grounded action planning, not fantasy or guaranteed transformation. |

## Reflective Flow

1. What change feels personally worth doing?
2. Why does it matter to the user?
3. What would make the user feel more capable?
4. What desire is the action serving?
5. What avoidance or friction may block it?
6. What future-self direction does it support?
7. What is the smallest concrete next action?
8. What if-then plan makes the action easier to do?

## Wording Rules

- Use "might", "could", and "does this fit?" rather than definitive psychological claims.
- Use "barrier", "friction", "context", or "pattern" rather than diagnosis or pathology.
- Use "reduced version" or "reset" rather than failure.
- Avoid guaranteed change, therapeutic language, and clinical labels.

## Evidence And Limits

The research report supports autonomous motivation, competence, relatedness, if-then planning, obstacle planning, and cue-based habit support as useful mechanisms. The exact BecomingOS feature design remains an implementation inference that must be validated with BecomingOS users before broad product claims.
