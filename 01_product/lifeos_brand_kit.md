# LifeOS Brand Kit

## 1. Purpose

This is the single source of truth for LifeOS brand direction. It consolidates the useful recommendations from the existing brand, wording, product, safety, and prototype documents into one canonical guide.

This document does not approve the MVP build, does not replace legal or safety review, and does not claim the brand direction is final where contradictions remain. It should guide product copy, visual design, design briefs, asset generation, and future brand review.

## 2. Brand Positioning

### Brand Essence

LifeOS is a calm, structured, private personal operating system. It helps people turn reflection, goals, decisions, patterns, support, and daily action into a system they can actually use.

### One-Line Description

LifeOS is a personal operating system for self-awareness, goals, decisions, reflection, support, review, and daily action.

### Short Product Description

LifeOS helps people map who they are, what matters, where they are going, and what to do next through guided reflection, AI-assisted insight, user-controlled memory, goals, reviews, and daily planning.

### Who It Is For

LifeOS is for people who want a private, practical system for understanding themselves and acting with more clarity. It is for reflective users, founders, carers, parents, operators, students, and anyone trying to organise goals, patterns, values, decisions, and daily behaviour without turning their life into a gamified productivity contest.

### What It Is Not

LifeOS is not therapy, diagnosis, medical treatment, crisis support, legal advice, financial advice, investment advice, or a replacement for qualified professional help. It is not a motivational hype product, hustle app, clinical assessment tool, generic chatbot, or generic blue SaaS dashboard.

## 3. Brand Principles

- Calm not boring: the product should reduce noise while still feeling alive, warm, and useful.
- Premium not flashy: quality should come from restraint, spacing, language, and material feel, not loud gradients or luxury cliches.
- Structured not cold: the system should feel organised and intelligent without feeling bureaucratic or clinical.
- Intelligent not clinical: LifeOS can notice patterns and offer reflection, but should avoid diagnostic language and medical framing.
- Supportive not therapeutic: the voice can be warm and steady, but must not imply treatment, therapy, monitoring, or professional care.
- Practical not motivational hype: copy should help the user choose a next action, not cheerlead with empty transformation language.
- AI-assisted not AI-controlled: AI can suggest, draft, summarise, and reflect. The user confirms, edits, rejects, and remains in control.
- Private not performative: the brand should feel like a personal operating room, not a social platform or content engine.
- Human not cute: warmth should come from respect and clarity, not cartoon graphics, infantilising copy, or forced playfulness.

## 4. Voice and Tone

### Voice Traits

LifeOS should sound calm, clear, direct, intelligent, warm, practical, non-shaming, autonomy-supportive, and lightly elegant. It should feel like a thoughtful guide who is careful with claims and careful with people.

It should not sound parental, clinical by default, salesy, cutesy, guru-like, corporate, or like a productivity drill sergeant.

### Writing Rules

- Use plain words and active voice.
- Keep sentences short where possible.
- Say what the system noticed before suggesting what it might mean.
- Use invitations before directives.
- Use "may", "might", "could", and "it seems" for AI interpretations.
- Offer clear exits: skip, pause, edit, reject, delete, or come back later.
- Acknowledge effort specifically rather than praising generically.
- Explain privacy, AI, memory, and safety boundaries at the moment they matter.
- Use action labels that describe the consequence of the tap.
- Avoid implying the AI knows the user's inner truth.

## 5. Logo Direction

### Preferred Logo Concept

The preferred direction is the OS Stack / layered system mark: three rounded horizontal layers forming a subtle "L" or personal operating system symbol.

This direction is preferred because it is app-friendly, calm, scalable, less generic than a compass, and directly communicates layers, system, structure, hierarchy, and user-controlled organisation.

### Alternative Logo Concepts

- Core Ring: a circular mark with a central dot and orbiting segments. It suggests the person at the centre with life areas around them.
- Compass Grid: a minimal compass or star inside a rounded square. It suggests direction and navigation.
- System Ring: a circular motif for progress, identity maps, and onboarding.
- Layered Cards: a motif for Identity, Values, Goals, Actions, Reviews, and Memory.

### Wordmark Rules

Use `LifeOS` as the preferred wordmark: capital L, capital OS, no space. "Life" may use regular or medium weight; "OS" may use semi-bold.

Avoid `Life OS`, `lifeOS`, `LIFEOS`, and `Life Os` in brand-facing contexts unless a product owner explicitly chooses a different naming system.

### App Icon Rules

The app icon should use the OS Stack mark inside a softly rounded square or squircle. It should remain legible at small sizes, work in one colour, and avoid tiny internal details.

Use Deep Ink or Olive Black on Warm Bone for the primary icon. A Sage or Muted Gold accent may be used sparingly.

### Logo Usage Rules

- Keep the mark simple enough to work at 16px.
- Use generous clear space around the mark.
- Prefer flat colour over gradients.
- Use the mark and wordmark together for landing, onboarding, and brand boards.
- Use the mark alone for app icon, favicon, compact nav, and social avatars.

### What To Avoid

- Generic linked rings as the primary direction.
- Medical crosses, brain icons, therapy couches, stethoscopes, hearts-as-healthcare marks, or diagnostic imagery.
- Generic blue SaaS marks.
- Cartoon mascots.
- Busy compass illustrations.
- Logos that imply AI control, surveillance, treatment, or crisis monitoring.

### Dark Mode Notes

Dark mode is mentioned as a future adaptability concern, not a settled palette. If added, it should use deep green-black surfaces, warm off-white text, restrained Sage and Muted Gold accents, and high-contrast borders. Do not invert into harsh black, neon green, or generic tech blue.

### Accessibility Notes

All text, controls, focus states, and semantic states must meet WCAG AA contrast. Do not rely on colour alone for status. Use labels, icons, and text for confirmation states, errors, warnings, and AI confidence.

## 7. Typography

### Primary UI Font

Use Inter for app UI, body text, forms, buttons, dashboards, navigation, controls, and dense product surfaces.

### Display Font

Use Fraunces or Cormorant Garamond for landing page hero headings, brand moments, quotes, and high-level editorial sections. Use sparingly.

### Fallback Fonts

- UI/body: `Inter, system-ui, sans-serif`
- Display: `Fraunces, Georgia, serif`
- Safest app-only fallback: Inter everywhere

### Usage Rules

- Use Inter everywhere inside the product unless a deliberate brand moment needs display type.
- Keep line length readable and line height generous.
- Avoid tiny grey text for safety, privacy, or consent copy.
- Avoid decorative type in forms, dense dashboards, and settings.
- Do not rely on display fonts for clarity-critical UI labels.

## 8. UI Design Direction

### App Feel

LifeOS should feel like a calm command centre, a private journal, a decision dashboard, and a structured personal map. It should feel warm, private, intelligent, premium, practical, and focused.

It should not look like a medical app, therapy app, finance dashboard, gamified habit tracker, bright social app, or generic productivity dashboard.

### Card Style

Cards should feel structured and quiet. Use warm surfaces, fine borders, modest shadows, and enough spacing to make the product calm without becoming sparse.

Preferred card direction:

```css
border-radius: 20px;
background: #EFE6D8;
border: 1px solid #DDD1C0;
box-shadow: 0 12px 30px rgba(23, 32, 27, 0.06);
```

### Button Style

- Primary: Olive Black background, Warm Bone text, rounded pill or calm rounded rectangle.
- Secondary: transparent or Warm Bone background, Olive Black text, Line Sand border.
- Accent: Muted Gold background, Deep Ink text, used sparingly.
- Destructive: Clay, used carefully with explicit copy.

### Navigation Style

Navigation should use clear labels, minimal icons, and enough breathing room. It should help the user understand the operating system structure without turning the product into a maze.

Recommended navigation language:

- Today
- Calendar
- Ask AI
- Goals
- My OS
- Review
- Support
- Settings

### Spacing, Radius, And Shadow Guidance

- Use generous spacing around reflective content.
- Use tighter spacing for repeated operational lists.
- Use radii from 10px to 24px; use pill radius for primary CTAs if the overall UI supports it.
- Use soft shadows sparingly.
- Avoid heavy glassmorphism, neon glow, large blur blobs, and decorative gradients.

### Mobile-First Guidance

Mobile screens should keep one primary action visible, avoid dense multi-column layouts, and preserve text readability. Reflection, consent, safety, and memory confirmation flows must be easy to complete with one hand and should not hide skip, pause, edit, or reject controls.

### Accessibility Notes

Use clear focus states, keyboard-accessible controls, visible labels, semantic headings, plain-language errors, and screen-reader-friendly button names. Avoid relying on audio, colour, animation, or AI output alone.

## 11. AI And Safety Brand Rules

### What LifeOS Can Say

- "This may help you reflect."
- "This is a suggested pattern."
- "You can confirm, edit, or reject this."
- "You stay in control."
- "Only confirmed or edited memories are eligible to guide AI."
- "This is not medical, legal, financial, therapy, or crisis advice."
- "For high-stakes decisions, consider qualified professional support."

### What LifeOS Must Not Say

- "We diagnose you."
- "We know what you need."
- "This will heal you."
- "This replaces therapy."
- "This is a crisis service."
- "The AI understands you better than you do."
- "Follow this treatment plan."
- "This is the right financial/legal/medical decision."
- "You will transform if you use this."

### AI Confirmation Language

AI outputs should be framed as drafts or suggestions. Use language like:

"AI suggestions are here to help you reflect, not to define you. Review, edit, or reject anything that does not feel accurate."

### Memory Confirmation Language

Use explicit user control:

"Save this to My OS? Only save this if it feels accurate and useful. You can edit, archive, or delete it later."

Rejected, deleted, draft, and archived memories must not be described as influencing AI.

### Crisis / Support Boundary Language

Use calm, direct wording:

"If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services now. LifeOS cannot provide emergency monitoring or crisis response."

For non-immediate concerns, LifeOS may provide support options and encourage the user to contact qualified professionals or relevant local services.

### Non-Clinical Positioning Rules

LifeOS can support reflection, planning, self-awareness, values clarification, daily action, and user-controlled memory. It must not diagnose, treat, monitor emergencies, replace care, promise outcomes, or provide regulated professional advice.

## 15. Recommendations

### Recommended Final Logo Direction

Use the OS Stack / layered system mark as the recommended direction. Keep Core Ring and Compass Grid as secondary exploration paths only if the OS Stack fails in real logo testing.

### Recommended Final Palette

Use the LifeOS warm premium palette: Deep Ink, Warm Bone, Soft Sand, Olive Black, Sage, Muted Gold, Mist Green, Clay, Dust Blue, Soft Charcoal, and Line Sand.

Do not use the generic app-kit blue/lavender/coral palette as the primary LifeOS palette.

### Recommended Typography

Use Inter for the product UI. Use Fraunces or Cormorant Garamond sparingly for display and brand moments. Use Inter everywhere if implementation speed, accessibility, or consistency is the priority.

### Recommended UI Style

Build toward a calm personal command centre: warm surfaces, soft structure, clear hierarchy, thin outline icons, user-controlled AI, and practical next steps. Avoid clinical styling, generic SaaS patterns, excessive cards-within-cards, loud gradients, and gamified habit-tracker energy.

### Recommended Next Design Tasks

- Produce OS Stack logo explorations in one-colour, app-icon, and wordmark lockup formats.
- Test the LifeOS wordmark against the current `lifeOS` lowercase variant used in existing product documents and app copy.

- Create a landing page concept using the brand board and safety language.
- Review all user-facing copy for non-clinical claims and user-control language.

### Unresolved Questions

- Should the public brand be `LifeOS`, `lifeOS`, or `BecomingOS`?
- The current app navigation now uses `Goals`, `Support`, and `My OS`; product review should confirm whether the underlying destinations should be renamed more deeply or only labelled this way at the navigation layer.
- Should display typography be introduced in the app, or reserved for marketing pages only?
- What launch geography controls crisis/support wording?
- Who is the final brand owner for approval?

- Some brand files are generic app kits, not LifeOS-specific kits. They recommend Linked Rings, blue/lavender/coral colours, Poppins display type, and generic app labels such as Home, Tasks, Messages, and Insights. These conflict with the LifeOS-specific warm OS direction.
- The LifeOS-specific kits recommend `LifeOS`, while existing product/app documents also use `lifeOS` and `BecomingOS`.
- Earlier docs describe the repository as documentation-first and not production-ready, while current app work contains a runnable private alpha. The brand kit should not be used as build approval.
- Prototype and PRD documents contain scope tension between low-fidelity prototype, MVP candidate, and broader module scope.
- Crisis examples in prototype material include Australia-specific resources, while final launch geography and localisation are not settled.
- Generic app kits recommend broader productivity/messaging/news hub positioning, which is weaker and less specific than the personal operating system positioning.
