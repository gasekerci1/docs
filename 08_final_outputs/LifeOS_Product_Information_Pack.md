# 3. One-Line Product Description

LifeOS is a personalised AI operating system that learns a user’s values, goals, memories, responsibilities, habits, relationships, priorities, calendar, and life signals, then uses that context to help them make better decisions, plan their day, stay accountable, and become their future self.

# 4. Plain English Product Summary

LifeOS is not just a planner, journal, habit tracker, chatbot, calendar app, or self-improvement tool.

LifeOS is a personalised AI system for a person’s life.

It learns who the user is, what matters to them, what they want to change, what responsibilities they carry, what goals they are chasing, what patterns hold them back, who supports them, and what kind of future version of themselves they want to become.

The app guides the user through self-discovery modules, captures their answers, creates a living memory system, and builds a personalised Future Self profile. The AI then uses that background context to answer questions, guide decisions, plan the user’s day, remind them of what matters, and help them act in alignment with their values.

Over time, LifeOS becomes a personalised “super brain” for the user: a decision engine, memory system, daily planner, accountability tool, and future-self guide.

# 5. Core Product Idea

The core product is the AI.

The app exists to teach the AI who the user is.

LifeOS is:

> A personalised AI brain powered by the user’s memories, answers, values, goals, calendar, responsibilities, relationships, behaviour patterns, and permissioned connected data.

A normal AI assistant gives general advice.

LifeOS gives personalised advice based on the user’s actual life context.

Example:

A normal AI says:

> You should focus on your most important goals today.

LifeOS says:

> You said rebuilding your business, spending more time with your kids, and improving your health are your top priorities. Your calendar today is full of admin tasks, but none of them move those priorities forward. I recommend blocking 90 minutes for business outreach, 30 minutes for movement, and scheduling time with your kids this weekend.

# 9. Product Philosophy

LifeOS is built around five principles.

## 9.1 The AI Is the Core

The main experience is asking the AI questions and receiving personalised answers based on accumulated user context.

## 9.2 The User Owns Their Data

The user controls what is stored, edited, deleted, connected, and used.

## 9.3 The App Must Be Explainable

When LifeOS gives advice, it explains what information influenced the answer.

Example:

> I’m recommending this because you marked family as a top priority, your calendar shows no family time this week, and you previously said you feel guilty when work takes over.

## 9.4 The System Builds the Future Self

The app should not only reflect who the user is now. It should help them become the person they said they want to become.

## 9.5 Automation Requires Permission

LifeOS can learn passively from connected data, but only with clear user consent.

# 10. Core Product Loop

The core LifeOS loop is:

1. User answers questions.
2. LifeOS stores approved context.
3. AI builds a personal profile.
4. User asks LifeOS for help.
5. AI gives personalised guidance.
6. User acts or rejects the guidance.
7. LifeOS learns from the result.
8. The next recommendation becomes more accurate.

This loop is the product.

Expanded product loop:

1. Teach LifeOS through onboarding and modules.
2. Confirm memories before they become durable context.
3. Ask LifeOS questions from the main AI surface.
4. Receive answers grounded in personal context.
5. Review the context used.
6. Accept, edit, reject, or correct the answer.
7. Save useful insights.
8. Use Today Plan and Decision Engine outputs in real life.
9. Review outcomes.
10. Improve future plans and answers through feedback.

## 12.2 AI Answer Requirements

Every major AI answer includes:

1. Direct answer.
2. Personal reasoning.
3. Context references.
4. Risks or trade-offs.
5. Recommended next action.
6. Option for user correction.

Example structure:

```text
Recommendation:
Do X.

Why:
This matches your stated priority of Y and your goal of Z.

Context I used:
- You said family is a top priority.
- Your calendar has no family time this week.
- You previously said you feel guilty when work takes over.

Next step:
Block 2 hours on Saturday afternoon.

Was this accurate?
[Accurate] [Partly] [Wrong] [Edit context]
```

## 12.3 User Correction Loop

The AI allows the user to correct answers.

Correction buttons:

* Accurate
* Partly accurate
* Wrong
* Edit memory
* Do not use this again
* Save this insight
* Delete this insight

Corrections improve future recommendations.

## 12.4 AI Interpretation Pattern

When LifeOS interprets user answers, it follows this product pattern:

1. User answers a module question or set of questions.
2. LifeOS drafts a summary or suggested pattern.
3. LifeOS asks whether the summary feels accurate.
4. User confirms, edits, rejects, skips, or saves.
5. Confirmed or edited material becomes usable memory.

Example:

```text
I noticed a possible pattern:
“You often delay health tasks when business pressure increases.”

Save this to memory?
[Save] [Edit] [Not now] [Never save this]
```

## 14.2 Onboarding Research Principles

The onboarding materials support:

* Adult learning principles: users need relevance, autonomy, clear purpose, and immediate usefulness.
* Microlearning: lessons stay short, focused, and easy to complete.
* Progressive disclosure: essential information appears first; deeper detail is available when needed.
* Multimedia clarity: visuals, text, and examples support each other instead of overwhelming the user.
* Universal Design for Learning: users can engage through different formats and accessibility needs.
* Reflection-based learning: users connect the concept to their own life.
* Completion motivation: users see progress and understand what module was installed.
* Survey fatigue reduction: the question load stays purposeful and non-repetitive.

## 14.3 Lesson Template

Each onboarding or module lesson can use this structure:

1. Lesson title.
2. Plain-language explanation.
3. Short text or short video.
4. Example or scenario.
5. Reflection question.
6. Optional confidence or alignment rating.
7. Free-text answer.
8. AI interpretation draft.
9. Confirm/edit/reject controls.
10. Save to memory.
11. Engine installed progress indicator.
12. Completion screen.

## 14.4 Onboarding Tone

Onboarding feels:

* deep but not heavy
* premium but not cold
* structured but not like school
* reflective but not intrusive
* calm, clear, and autonomy-supportive
* practical rather than motivational hype

## 14.5 Survey Fatigue Controls

Question flows use:

* clear purpose for each question
* short modules
* limited repetitive prompts
* mixed question types
* optional free-text where possible
* progress indicators
* skip or “not now” options
* confirmation before memories are saved

# 16. Main User Flows

## 16.1 Self-Discovery Flow

1. User opens a module.
2. User answers guided questions.
3. AI summarises key insights.
4. User approves, edits, or rejects insights.
5. Approved insights become memories.
6. Module progress updates.
7. Future Self becomes more complete.

## 16.2 Ask LifeOS Flow

1. User asks a question.
2. System retrieves relevant memories, goals, priorities, calendar, and signals.
3. AI generates personalised answer.
4. Answer references used context.
5. User accepts, corrects, or rejects.
6. Corrections update the memory system.

## 16.3 Today Plan Flow

1. User opens Today.
2. System checks calendar, goals, tasks, responsibilities, health signals, and priorities.
3. AI generates daily plan.
4. User accepts or edits plan.
5. Plan can create tasks or calendar blocks with approval.
6. End-of-day review captures what happened.

## 16.4 Decision Flow

1. User enters decision.
2. User adds options if needed.
3. LifeOS compares options against values, goals, priorities, risks, and future self.
4. AI recommends option.
5. User can ask follow-up questions.
6. Decision and outcome can be saved.

## 16.5 Support Circle Flow

1. User adds support person.
2. User defines permissions.
3. User creates accountability rule.
4. LifeOS prompts user when relevant.
5. User approves any outgoing message.
6. All actions are logged.

## 16.6 Memory Confirmation Flow

1. AI identifies a possible memory.
2. LifeOS shows the source or reason for the memory suggestion.
3. User chooses save, edit, not now, or never save.
4. Saved memory becomes usable context.
5. Rejected memory is not used.
6. Edited memory replaces the AI draft.

## 16.7 Review Flow

1. User reviews the day or week.
2. LifeOS asks what happened.
3. User marks actions complete, missed, reduced, moved, or no longer relevant.
4. LifeOS asks what helped and what got in the way.
5. AI identifies lessons or patterns.
6. User decides what becomes memory.
7. LifeOS adjusts future plans.

# 22. Behaviour Change and Research Foundations

## 22.1 Evidence-Based Growth Loop

LifeOS uses this product loop:

```text
diagnosis flow -> Today Plan -> if-then plan -> completion tracking -> failure diagnosis -> adaptive reset
```

Product meaning:

1. Identify the real barrier.
2. Choose one feasible action.
3. Attach the action to a cue or context.
4. Track whether it happened.
5. Learn what got in the way.
6. Adjust the next plan.

## 22.2 COM-B

COM-B is used as a behavioural lens:

* Capability: knowledge, skill, energy, or confidence.
* Opportunity: time, environment, resources, social context, or friction.
* Motivation: value alignment, desire, avoidance, competing priorities, or preparedness.

LifeOS uses this to avoid giving the wrong kind of plan for the wrong kind of barrier.

## 22.3 Self-Determination Theory

LifeOS supports:

* autonomy through user choice and correction
* competence through realistic actions and reduced-plan options
* relatedness through optional Support Circle and relationship-aware planning

## 22.4 Implementation Intentions

LifeOS turns intentions into if-then plans:

```text
If it is [time/cue/context] and [condition], then I will [specific action].
```

## 22.5 WOOP / MCII

LifeOS can use obstacle-aware planning:

* Wish
* Outcome
* Obstacle
* Plan

## 22.6 Habit and Cue Design

LifeOS supports habit formation through:

* stable cues
* specific actions
* lower-friction environments
* repetition
* self-monitoring
* feedback
* plan adjustment
* reduced shame after missed actions

## 22.7 Bad Day Mode

Bad Day Mode creates a lower-friction version of the plan.

It may include:

* smaller version
* shorter duration
* easier context
* delayed timing
* minimum viable action
* reset plan

Bad Day Mode keeps the user moving without treating a hard day as failure.

# 34. Product Risk Areas

## 34.1 Too Much Scope

Risk:

Trying to include every possible feature at once.

Product response:

Keep the centre of the product clear: AI, memory, Future Self, Today Plan, and decisions.

## 34.2 Weak Personalisation

Risk:

AI feels generic.

Product response:

Every major answer uses stored user context and shows context used.

## 34.3 Privacy Concerns

Risk:

Users do not trust the system.

Product response:

Data Control Centre, permissioned integrations, explainable AI, and memory approval.

## 34.4 Unsafe Claims

Risk:

Product sounds like therapy, crisis support, or emergency monitoring.

Product response:

Clear boundaries and safe language.

## 34.5 Bad Memory Quality

Risk:

The AI stores wrong or overconfident memories.

Product response:

Memory confirmation, editing, source tracking, and confidence scores.

## 34.6 Autonomous Action Risk

Risk:

AI sends messages, contacts people, or changes calendar without approval.

Product response:

Human approval is required for external actions.

# 35. Final Product Definition

LifeOS is a personalised AI operating system for life.

It learns from the user’s answers, memories, goals, values, responsibilities, relationships, life events, calendar, and permissioned connected signals. It uses that context to help the user ask better questions, make better decisions, plan their time, stay accountable, and become the future version of themselves.

The AI is the centre of the product.

The modules teach the AI.

The memory engine gives the AI continuity.

The Future Self gives the AI direction.

The Decision Engine turns context into choices.

The Today Plan turns choices into action.

The Life Signals make the system smarter over time.

The Data Control Centre keeps the user in control.

The long-term goal is to create a trusted personal intelligence layer that helps people live with more clarity, alignment, discipline, and purpose.
